1. What Initial Estimate is

Initial Estimate is the step where DocuSketch turns a documented property into a real scope: a list of line items with codes, quantities, units and prices, grouped by the room each one belongs to — ready to review, edit and export to your estimating software.

It runs in the web portal, on the project you already captured. You do not write the first draft: the estimate is generated from what is already in the project — the sketch, the rooms you marked as affected, the scope of work, the 360° tour, photos and documents. What you do is review it, correct what needs correcting, and send it on.

Why it is different from a spreadsheet

Every line item keeps a trail back to the thing that produced it. Open any line and you can see which room it belongs to, what evidence drove it — a photo, a room measurement, a note in the scope of work — and how the quantity was calculated, down to the arithmetic. Nothing is a number you have to take on faith, which is what makes the estimate defensible when somebody on the other side of the claim asks why a line is there.

What you can do here

  • Generate a first set of line items, and choose how broad that set should be.
  • Review the scope room by room, with prices and totals updating as you work.
  • Edit anything: codes, descriptions, quantities, the room a line belongs to.
  • Inspect the evidence and the quantity calculation behind each line.
  • Accept or ignore the additional items DocuSketch suggests.
  • Clear the required actions that stand between the estimate and an export.
  • Export the result, or keep iterating in a new version.
Where it sits in the workflow

Initial Estimate is the last stop of the documentation chain — capture, mark, scope, estimate — so how completely the project was documented decides how good the generated scope is.

2. Before you start

An Initial Estimate can only be as good as the project behind it, so the portal walks you through four steps. Only the first one is required — with a sketch in place you can generate immediately. The two in the middle are optional, and they are what sharpen the result.

  1. Get a sketch Capture the property with Instant Sketch in the DocuSketch mobile app, or order an Enhanced sketch. The result is a floor plan with measurements — the geometry every quantity is calculated from.
  2. Mark affected areas (optional, but it sharpens the scope) Mark every room the loss touched, directly on the sketch, and set the water category and class where they apply. The scope follows what you mark.
  3. Review your scope (optional) Confirm that rooms, materials and quantities look right before estimating. Corrections are much cheaper here than in the line-item table.
  4. Generate the initial estimate Takes a few seconds, and you can re-run it. What comes back is a Xactimate-ready scope, ready to review and export.

The screen tracks these for you: each step carries its own state — Done, Optional or Ready — and the heading changes to You're ready to generate your estimate as soon as nothing is blocking.

Opening it

Open the project and go to the Estimates tab. That tab tracks every estimate on the project — the ones you generate yourself and the ones ordered from the DocuSketch expert team. Use Create Instant Estimate to start a new one, or open an existing Initial estimate row to go back into the editor.

Availability

Initial Estimate depends on your company plan and on your user permissions. If you do not see the tab, or the generate action is unavailable, contact your DocuSketch account manager.

3. How it works, end to end

Once the project is ready, working an Initial Estimate is a loop of five moves. You will rarely do them strictly in order — most people jump between reviewing and editing — but this is the shape of it.

StepWhat happensWhere it is covered
GenerateYou choose how broad the generated scope should be, and DocuSketch builds the line items.Section 4
ReviewYou read the scope room by room and check the lines you are unsure about.Section 6, 7
EditYou correct codes and quantities, add what the capture missed, and remove what does not belong.Section 8
ClearYou work through the required actions — the flags that stand between the estimate and an export.Section 9
ExportYou take the estimate out to your estimating software.Section 11
Your work is versioned

You never lose a previous state. Every generation creates a new version, and every saved round of edits a new revision inside it — so you can compare, and go back. See Versions and revisions.

4. Generating a version

When you generate, you choose a scope strategy: how much DocuSketch should put in the first draft. The three strategies are nested — each one contains everything the previous one has, plus more — so the choice is really about where you want to start: with the minimum you can defend line by line, or with the widest set, which you then prune.

StrategyIn one lineWhat it includes
Documented Direct data only Line items pulled strictly from provided data — scope of work, comments, 360°, documents and Instant Sketch.
Standard Adds customer profile Everything in Documented, plus items carried over from the customer profile — your office defaults and standards.
Expanded Includes AI predictions The largest output, including AI predictions and suggestions. Review and prune before committing.

Choosing with the numbers in front of you

The picker does not make you guess. Each strategy shows how many line items it would produce; the selected one is broken down by confidence — how many high, medium and low — and a short Adds, e.g. list names example items it brings in over the narrower option. The office's own estimate total sits beside the button, so you can see what you are about to apply.

Nothing is committed until you press Use Documented, Use Standard or Use Expanded. Until then the table is read-only — the banner says the estimate is ready and waiting for you to pick a scope to unlock editing.

While it runs

Generating takes seconds, and you are not locked out while it happens: a status strip reports progress and tells you that you can keep working. Results stream into the table as they arrive.

Changing your mind

A committed scope is not a dead end. If the version was generated with the wrong strategy, Back to original version in the header reopens the original generated set so you can pick a different scope. Generating again creates a new version and leaves the current one untouched.

Your office has a default

The strategy the picker opens on is set in your office's estimate profile, so your team starts from the same place every time. You can always switch for a single estimate.

5. Versions and revisions

Estimates are numbered Version N.R — the version number changes when a new estimate is generated, the revision number when you save edits to it. The project's Estimates tab is where they all live: every initial estimate on the project, with its status, how many line items it holds, its amount, any notes, and when it was created and last updated.

Reading the list

  • Tabs across the top split the list by estimate type — All, Emergency/Mitigation, Contents, Reconstruction — each with its own count.
  • The status badge tells you where the estimate stands: New for one nobody has worked yet, Done once it has been completed, and the signing states — Awaiting signature, Signed — for estimates sent out for approval.
  • The row itself names the version and the number of line items, for example Initial estimate v12 · 98 line items.
  • The history icon opens what happened to that estimate over time; the arrow opens the editor.

When to generate a new version instead of editing

Edit the current version when you are correcting it: a wrong quantity, a missing item, a line in the wrong room. Generate a new version when the basis changed — you marked more affected areas, reworked the scope of work, or want to see what a broader strategy would produce. The old version stays on the project, so you can compare the two and send whichever you prefer.

6. Reading the line-item table

The table is the estimate. Items are grouped, each group carries its own subtotal, and the footer carries the preliminary total for the version.

The columns

ColumnWhat it tells you
#The line number, and a dot that marks where the line came from.
CAT / SEL / ActThe estimating code: category, selector and activity.
DescriptionThe line item itself, in plain words.
CalcThe expression the quantity is derived from, where one applies.
Qty / UnitHow much, in which unit of measure.
Unit Price / TotalPricing for the line, and the line total.
SourceWhether the line came from DocuSketch or was added by a person.
ConfHow confident DocuSketch is in the quantity on this line.
Row actionsAdd a line below, delete the line, or open its detail panel.

How rows are grouped

Groups nest the way the property does: Project at the top, then Level — First Floor, Second Floor — then Room. Every group header carries its item count and its own total, and collapses so you can work one room at a time. Items that are not assigned to a room yet sit directly under Project — a standing signal that they still need a home.

The controls above the table

  • QTY confidence — filter the table down to the quantities DocuSketch is least sure of, instead of reading all of them.
  • Search items — find a line by code or description without scrolling.
  • Add line item and Add room — see Editing line items.
  • Pricing: Min / Avg / Max — switch the whole estimate between the low, average and high price tier. Totals update as you switch.
  • AI suggestions — additional items DocuSketch thinks belong here, with a count. See AI assistance.
  • Required actions — the count of flags still open, and the way into the report. See Clearing required actions.
  • Export — the way out. See Exporting and sharing.
Preliminary total

The figure in the footer is labelled preliminary for a reason: it moves as you edit quantities, switch the pricing tier and accept suggestions. It is a working number, not a quote.

7. Where a line item came from

Select any line item and its detail panel opens beside the table. Besides the editable fields, it answers the two questions that matter when the estimate is challenged: what evidence put this line here, and how was this quantity arrived at.

The header of the panel

Three facts sit at the top before any detail: the line number, the source — DS Engine for a line DocuSketch produced, User for one a person added — and the quantity confidence, High, Medium or Low. Below them the code and description, and the room the line belongs to.

AI Justification Summary

This block lists the evidence the line was driven by, one row per source. A row names where the evidence came from — the sketch, a field note, the 360° tour — and what was observed there. An equipment line, for example, cites the sketch and reads Dehumidifier refrigerant, Air mover centrifugal, Air mover axial — equipment present.

Quantity Justification

Three labelled rows explain the number in the Qty column:

  • How determined — whether the quantity was measured from the sketch, taken from the scope, or predicted.
  • Confidence — High, Medium or Low, with a coloured dot, matching the Conf column in the table.
  • Calculation — the arithmetic in full, written out.

The calculation is the part worth reading when a quantity surprises you. A drying-labour line, for instance, spells out both candidate figures and which one won: equipment monitoring 35 units × 2 hr/day × 3 days = 6 hr; area 549.02 sq ft / 1,000 × 3 days = 1.65 hr; using the larger = 6 hr. If you disagree with the result, you can see exactly which input to argue with.

Source notes and your own justification

Where a line traces back to something somebody said on site, the note is quoted in full under Source Notes — the technician's own words about the room, the damage and what they decided. You can also attach your own reasoning to a line with Add justification, which is the right place to record a judgement call that the evidence alone does not explain.

8. Editing line items

Everything in the table is yours to change. Edits are held until you save them, so you can work through a whole room and commit it in one go — or discard the round if it went wrong. The toolbar shows Unsaved changes while anything is pending.

Editing in place

Click a cell and type. Codes, description, calculation, quantity and unit price are all editable directly in the row; the same fields sit in the detail panel if you prefer to work there, where Previous and Next walk you through the estimate line by line.

The Calc field

Calc is how you express a quantity instead of hard-coding it. It takes a plain number (12), a formula (12+8), or a variable the sketch provides:

VariableMeansUnit
FFloor areaSF
CCeiling areaSF
WWall areaSF
PFFloor perimeterLF
PCCeiling perimeterLF

You can combine them with + - * / ( ), and a number next to a variable multiplies it — 0.41C means 0.41 × ceiling area. The advantage over a typed number is that the quantity stays tied to the measured room: the cheat-sheet sits next to the field so you do not have to memorise it.

Adding items

Add line item opens the price catalog. Search it by code or description, and add what you need. The panel states where the item will land — Adding to: Project, or the room you picked — and lets you change that destination before you add, so new lines do not quietly pile up at the project level. Add room creates a room group when the scope needs one the sketch does not have.

Duplicating, moving, deleting

  • Duplicate copies a line — useful when the same work repeats in several rooms with different quantities.
  • Move reassigns a line to another room, which is how you clear items sitting under Project.
  • Delete removes it. Deleting is per line, and the row actions sit at the end of every row.
  • Tick several rows first and the toolbar offers the same actions in bulk.

Saving and discarding

Save commits the round and creates a new revision of the version. Discard throws the pending edits away and returns the table to the last saved state. Leaving the editor with unsaved changes prompts you first.

9. Clearing required actions

Before an estimate leaves the portal, DocuSketch tells you what still needs a decision. The toolbar keeps the count in view — 28 required actions — and clicking it opens the full report. Rows that need attention are highlighted in the table too, so you can work them in place.

What the report contains

The report groups everything by how much it matters, and says so at the top: errors should be fixed before export; warnings and suggested items are optional.

  • Errors — something is wrong with the line and the export will carry it. Fix these.
  • Warnings — most commonly a missing activity code (ACT) or a description that overrides the catalog default. Each one names the line number and its code, so you can tell at a glance whether it matters.
  • Unassigned items — lines with no room. They export, but nobody downstream can tell where the work happens.
  • Suggested items — things DocuSketch thinks are missing, offered rather than applied.

Working through them

Each entry offers Review, which takes you to that line in the table, and Ignore, which clears the flag without changing the line. Filter table to flagged rows hides everything else, which is the fastest way to clear a long list. Dismissed warnings are not gone forever — they come back if the condition that raised them returns.

Ignore is a decision, not a shortcut

Ignoring a warning records that you looked at it and chose to proceed. That is a reasonable thing to do for a code your office does not use — but it is worth a moment's thought on anything that affects a quantity or a price, because the export carries the line as it stands.

10. AI assistance

Beyond generating the scope, the editor offers three kinds of help. All of them propose; none of them change the estimate without you.

AI suggestions

The toolbar carries a count of AI suggestions — additional line items DocuSketch thinks belong in this estimate but did not add. Open it and they are listed the way the estimate is: by project, level and room, each with its code, description, quantity, price and confidence. Every row offers Add line item to accept it and Ignore to dismiss it. This is the safest way to widen a Documented scope: you see each candidate with its evidence rather than regenerating everything.

Ask DS AI

Describe a change in plain language and review what it proposes before anything is applied — add baseboard trim, swap vinyl for LVP, make cleaning heavy. It returns proposed line items, and you decide.

Ask Aime

An AI review of the estimate as it stands, for when you want a second opinion on the whole scope rather than a specific edit.

Availability

The AI assistants are rolled out per account. If you do not see them in the editor header, they are not enabled for your company yet.

11. Exporting and sharing

Export opens a panel titled Download or share this estimate. The estimate leaves DocuSketch as an ESX file — the format Xactimate reads — so the scope you reviewed here becomes the scope you continue with there.

Clear the blockers first

If anything still needs a decision, the panel opens on that instead of on the formats: a red count of items needing review, each one named by line number and code, with Review to work through them and Bypass all to accept them as they stand. You clear that list, then export.

Export options

Alongside the file, the panel offers what travels with it — line notes, and a documentation pack of the project's photos and reports. Options still being built are marked Coming soon in the panel, so what you see there is always the current truth.

Exporting does not freeze the estimate

The version stays editable after an export, and the project keeps every version. If you export and then spot a correction, fix it and export again — the earlier file is unaffected.

12. On a phone

Native app section pending

Opened on a phone, the portal switches to a layout built for one. Each line item becomes a card with its code, description, quantity and price; search, the pricing tier and the AI actions stack above the list; Add and Export are fixed to the bottom of the screen. A Saved indicator in the header tells you the state of your edits. Tapping a card opens that item's details, including its justifications.

This is the same estimate and the same data as on a desktop — handy for a walk-through on site, where you are checking the scope against the room you are standing in. Long review sessions are still easier on a desktop, where the table and the detail panel sit side by side.

13. FAQ and troubleshooting

To extend from support tickets

Why is a room missing from my estimate?

The scope follows the rooms marked as affected. If a room was never marked, nothing was generated for it. Mark it and generate a new version, or add the room and its items by hand in the editor.

Why does a line item have a quantity I did not expect?

Open the line and read Quantity Justification. It names how the quantity was determined and writes out the calculation, including which of two candidate figures was used. If the inputs are wrong, correct the Calc expression; if the method is wrong, override the quantity directly.

Why can I not export yet?

Items still need review. The export panel lists them with their line numbers — clear them, or bypass them deliberately, and the export proceeds.

I generated with the wrong scope strategy. What now?

Use Back to original version to reopen the original generated set and pick a different strategy, or generate a new version. Either way the current version stays on the project.

Why are so many items sitting under Project instead of a room?

Either they are genuinely project-wide, or the capture did not tie them to a room. Move them with the row action — and treat a long Project group as a sign that affected areas were marked thinly.

Why do prices differ from what my office normally uses?

Check the pricing tier — Min, Avg and Max change every line at once. Beyond that, pricing comes from your office's catalog, so a persistent difference is a catalog question rather than an estimate one.
